# Particle Physics in 17 Hours: a compact learning atlas

Main source: [Kamil Laurent's course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw).
Chapter starts below come from the creator metadata retrieved 19 October 2026.
This is an original synthesis guided by its chapter map, selected retrieved
captions and a previously saved whole-course distillation. It is not a claim
that every caption or chalkboard equation was independently audited this run.
The equations below use stated conventions; they are not slide transcriptions.

## The through-line

Describe the degrees of freedom and transformations, write an action, derive
the dynamics, calculate amplitudes and connect them to observations. Keep
fundamental structure separate from difficult calculation techniques. A model
of how particles behave is not a complete philosophical account of what exists.

For each lesson: explain → derive one step → test one limit → ask a transfer
question. The research bridge adds an explicit measurement model and alternatives.

## 1. Amplitudes before probabilities

[00:51:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=3116)
Probability amplitude, followed by operators, Schrödinger evolution and
superposition through 01:31:53.

Insight: coherent alternatives combine as amplitudes, then probabilities use
the squared magnitude. |a+b|² contains an interference term; |a|²+|b|² does not.
Do not add classical probabilities unless distinguishability, decoherence or
the measurement model justifies it. In a position representation, integration
of |ψ|² gives a probability under the appropriate normalization.

Exercise: take a=1/2 and b=-1/2. Compare the two expressions. Research bridge:
ask which phase information survives averaging before fitting a neutrino effect.

## 2. Symmetry, invariance and covariance

[01:43:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=6217)
Rotations; [02:02:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=7376)
invariance/covariance; Lorentz transformations at 02:11:13.

Insight: a component may change while the physical relation keeps its form.
With metric (+,-,-,-), a four-vector satisfies xμxμ = c²t²-|x|².
A frame change is not a new force. Fix coordinates, metric and index conventions
before comparing derivations. Maxwell's relativistic form is a bridge, not a
claim that the nonrelativistic Schrödinger equation is invalid in its own domain.

Exercise: identify an invariant and a frame-dependent quantity in a collision.
Research bridge: reject apparent discoveries caused by inconsistent frames.

## 3. Relativistic dynamics and natural units

[03:10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=11454)
Relativistic energy; natural units at 03:15:51; Klein–Gordon at 03:28:58.

Insight: E²=p²c²+m²c⁴ leads to a relativistic wave equation, but the interpretation
of its conserved current is not the naïve positive single-particle probability
density. Scalar field theory remains useful. Setting c=ħ=1 simplifies dimensions;
it does not erase conversion factors when comparing a result to metres or seconds.

Exercise: restore c in the mass term of the energy relation. Research bridge:
check dimensions before trusting a long simulation or an effective coupling.

## 4. Dirac spinors, spin and antiparticles

[03:39:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=13184)
Dirac equation; spin at 04:34:01; antiparticle interpretation at 04:56:02
and 05:18:11; spinor transformations at 05:36:20.

Insight: the spinor representation is part of the theory, not a cosmetic label.
In natural units, (iγμ∂μ-m)ψ=0 uses matrices obeying {γμ,γν}=2gμνI.
Do not describe antiparticles as ordinary observable objects with negative
physical energy. Spatial-spin SU(2) and weak-isospin SU(2) are not the same action.

Exercise: explain why a scalar and a spinor transform differently, without
claiming a spinor is a tiny classical spinning ball.

## 5. Fields and action make an interaction executable

[06:02:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=21732)
Fields; mechanics at 06:16:44; least action at 06:40:10; field Lagrangians
at 07:01:43.

Insight: scalar, vector and spinor fields assign differently transforming
objects across spacetime. Stationary action yields the field Euler–Lagrange
equation ∂μ[∂L/∂(∂μφ)]-∂L/∂φ=0, with boundary terms handled appropriately.
“Least action” is usually stationarity, not a guaranteed global minimum.

Exercise: derive the free real scalar equation from
L=(1/2)∂μφ∂μφ-(1/2)m²φ². Research bridge: replace a guessed rotation angle
with an actual Hamiltonian and its assumptions before searching for effects.

## 6. Quantization and particle number

[07:24:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=26674)
Field quantization; creation/annihilation at 07:45:45.

Insight: quantized field excitations support processes with changing particle
number; a fixed single-particle wavefunction is not sufficient for every
relativistic process. Bosonic and fermionic mode operators have different
commutation structures. A creation operator is not evidence an experiment
produced a particle; predictions still need a process and measurement.

Exercise: identify the conserved quantities and changing particle count in a
specified reaction rather than treating particle count as universally conserved.

## 7. From global phase symmetry to QED

[08:18:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=29899)
Global U(1); local symmetry at 08:39:25; QED at 09:05:52.

Insight: a spacetime-dependent phase requires a corresponding connection in
the covariant derivative. For ψ'=exp(iqα)ψ and Dμ=∂μ+iqAμ, use
A'μ=Aμ-∂μα. State this sign convention before comparing formulas. The full
electromagnetic theory also includes a gauge-field kinetic term. Local gauge
redundancy is not by itself an observable new force or free choice of couplings.

Exercise: expand D'μψ' and check covariance. Research bridge: demand complete
field content and the gauge kinetic terms, not an isolated attractive operator.

## 8. Feynman amplitudes are not literal trajectories

[09:37:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=34649)
Diagram building blocks; rules at 10:09:55; loops at 10:28:42 and 10:44:21.

Insight: diagrams organize perturbative contributions to amplitudes. A virtual
internal line is not a directly observed particle path. Loop calculations need
a regulator, renormalization prescription and consistency checks; an infinity
in an intermediate expression is not automatically an infinite observable.

Exercise: identify external states, a coupling order and a conserved quantum
number. Research bridge: check normalization, known limits and factorization,
not a single condition that an identically zero expression could also pass.

## 9. Weak interactions, SU(2) and chirality

[10:55:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=39308)
Beta decay; SU(2) at 11:20:40; charged weak interactions at 11:49:51;
gauge self-interactions at 12:09:35; chiral states at 12:30:27.

Insight: particle content and representations determine what a symmetry means.
In the Standard Model charged weak interaction, left-chiral fermion doublets
play a special role. Chirality and helicity are distinct concepts for massive
particles. A neutrino's propagation state and its detector interaction are
different stages; detecting flavour does not mean oscillations happen only
inside the detector.

Exercise: distinguish a change of mathematical basis from a physical state
evolution with a fixed measurement operator.

## 10. Electroweak structure and the Higgs mechanism

[12:56:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=46573)
Unification; SU(2)L×U(1)Y at 13:12:20; symmetry breaking at 13:52:33;
Higgs examples at 14:25:08 and 14:44:47; masses at 15:08:45 and 15:14:04.

Insight: the gauge-invariant theory and vacuum structure determine the broken-
phase mass relations. Gauge-boson masses from the scalar kinetic term and
fermion masses from Yukawa terms are related but different mechanisms. It is
misleading to explain all mass as literal friction against a medium. The
unbroken electromagnetic gauge symmetry leaves the photon massless in this model.

Exercise: distinguish a bare forbidden mass term from an allowed coupling that
produces a mass term after the scalar field is expanded around its vacuum value.

## 11. Quarks, colour and QCD

[15:38:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=56325)
Quarks; confinement/colour at 15:54:33; global SU(3)C at 16:07:41;
local QCD at 16:24:47.

Insight: colour is an internal quantum number, not a visible pigment. Non-Abelian
field strengths contain self-interactions. Writing a compact Lagrangian does
not make every bound-state observable easy to calculate, and perturbation
theory is not equally useful at all scales. A classical colour cartoon is
an analogy, not a diagram of a quark's physical paint.

Exercise: explain why gauge self-interaction differs from the simplest Abelian
case. Research bridge: choose an approximation whose validity matches the scale.

## 12. Put the Standard Model together; preserve its limits

[16:33:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YFiQIZOM2fw&t=59592)
Combined gauge structure; fermions and gauge interactions through 17:10:41;
masses/Yukawa terms at 17:15:16 and 17:20:05; generations/mixing at 17:23:09.

Insight: SU(3)C×SU(2)L×U(1)Y is a structural map, not a complete explanation
of every parameter or all of nature. Mixing involves relations between bases;
do not confuse a basis label with an independent physical population change.
The minimal renormalizable field content without right-handed neutrinos or
higher-dimensional mass operators does not explain observed neutrino mass
splittings. Gravity and dark-matter identification are not solved by writing
the Standard Model. Earth gravity lessons are a separate bridge, not content
claimed to be covered by this special-relativity course.

Exercise: write a “known structure / measured input / missing explanation” card.
Choose one gap for a research effort, not a demand that an agent solve all physics.

## Use the atlas in a research campaign

Pick one anchor, one unresolved question and one cheap falsifier. Examples:
chapter 5 → derive a candidate interaction → check a conserved quantity;
chapter 9 → specify a propagation model → expose an identifiability degeneracy;
chapter 8 → reproduce a small amplitude → test limits with an independent method.
