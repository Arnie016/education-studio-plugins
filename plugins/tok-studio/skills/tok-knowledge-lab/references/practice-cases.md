# Original practice cases

These short exercises were authored for TOK Studio. None is an official IB prescribed title, IA prompt, historical incident or a student's submission.

## The station clock

A station clock is usually maintained carefully but stopped yesterday. You glance at it at the one moment when it shows the correct time.

Question: Is the truth of your belief enough, or does the route by which you reached it matter?

Follow-up only after an answer: what would you check before trusting the same clock tomorrow? Explain this as a familiar epistemic-luck pattern, not an original philosophical discovery.

## The museum label

Two labels for the same artifact emphasize different parts of its documented history. One discusses craftsmanship; the other discusses who controlled its acquisition.

Question: What would make these interpretations compatible, conflicting or differently useful?

Do not infer that every interpretation is equally well supported.

## The class survey

Nine classmates prefer a new timetable. A student concludes that the entire school prefers it.

Question: Which part of the leap depends on how the nine students were selected?

Do not assume the survey was random, fabricated or representative. Ask for the sampling method.

## The edited screenshot

A screenshot displays a claim, but its author, date and surrounding conversation are missing.

Question: Which missing fact would most change whether you use it as evidence, and why?

Avoid declaring the screenshot fake merely because its context is absent.
